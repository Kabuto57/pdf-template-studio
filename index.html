The problems, and what changes

Problem todayWhat happens nowWith this demo
1. The form is hard to changeBuilt in a legacy Adobe tool (LiveCycle). Every change waits on a specialist and the file must be re-signed with Adobe's server.Each question is one line of text. Change it, the tests check it, and it is live in about 2 minutes.
2. Only works in AdobeEdge, Chrome, Mac Preview and phones show "Please wait…". Its checks need JavaScript, which many laptops have off.A standard PDF that opens in every viewer. Red frames, date lists and drop-downs guide the customer even with JavaScript off.
3. Forms arrive incomplete or wrongChecks only run when the customer presses Print. Ops finds the problems after it arrives, then goes back and forth.Problems are caught while filling in. Whatever slips through is flagged the moment ops opens it, with a ready-made reason to send back.
4. Ops reads, checks and retypes by handPrint, scan (barcodes or OCR), check against the customer's records by eye, retype into the system.Every answer read exactly from the PDF in seconds. Rules, cross-checks and the customer record checked automatically. Approve or reject, and submit.
5. OCR / AI cost and data leaving the bankEvery page goes through outsourced OCR.Filled PDFs need no OCR at all, and everything stays in the browser. OCR or AI is only needed for paper and scans.

What it saves, per application

Open any application in the workbench. The Done automatically bar counts the work an officer no longer does by hand. For the sample LC:

  • ~50 answers read from the PDF's fields, with none retyped and no OCR
  • ~35 mandatory questions and 44 conditional rules checked
  • ~45 typed answers checked for format, real dates, length and characters
  • 6 cross-answer checks: dates in order, amount, insurance against price basis
  • 5 details compared with the customer record (name, address, tel, account, signatory)

Try the eight sample applications: complete, missing answers, wrong formats, differs from the customer record, unknown customer (KYC), contradicting answers, and a printed copy whose fields were lost.
